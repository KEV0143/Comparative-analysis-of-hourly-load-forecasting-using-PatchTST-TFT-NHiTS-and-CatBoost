<h2 align="center" style="border-bottom: none; border: none;">Abstract</h2>

&emsp;&emsp;In modern conditions of dynamic demand behavior and external factor influences, traditional statistical autoregressive modeling methods (ARIMA, SARIMAX) often give way to machine learning algorithms (Gradient Boosting) and modern deep learning architectures (Transformers).

&emsp;&emsp;**Research Aim** – solving the problem of selecting the best power consumption forecasting model for each hour of the next day under conditions of limited information using modern machine and deep learning methods: CatBoost, Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT), PatchTST, N-HiTS.

&emsp;&emsp;**Materials and Methods.** The program code is written in Python and available open-source on GitHub at: https://github.com/KEV0143/Comparative-analysis-of-hourly-load-forecasting-using-PatchTST-TFT-NHiTS-and-CatBoost.

&emsp;&emsp;**Results.** Within each of the considered methods, new high-quality and adequate mathematical models for power consumption forecasting were developed. An important mathematical result is that solving the problem – electricity consumption forecasting for enterprise energy management – has been reduced to a scientifically grounded selection of a single best model. This is significant both for theoretical investigation of modern machine and deep learning algorithms and for their practical application, considering growing data volumes. The task is crucial for optimizing electricity consumption at both enterprise and regional levels.

&emsp;&emsp;**Conclusion.** As a result of the theoretical analysis and computational experiment conducted on real-world data from a Russian power supply company, an important practical conclusion was drawn regarding the applicability of the CatBoost model for optimizing enterprise electricity consumption. All conclusions presented in the article are confirmed by statistical test results.

&emsp;&emsp;**Keywords:** systems analysis of models, mathematical statistics, short-term forecasting, electricity consumption, comparative systems analysis, machine learning, deep learning, CatBoost, Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT), PatchTST, N-HiTS

<h2 align="center" style="border-bottom: none; border: none;">INTRODUCTION</h2>

&emsp;&emsp;Operational and short-term load forecasting (STLF) of electricity consumption is a critical task for market participants in the Wholesale Electricity and Capacity Market (WECM) [1]. High forecasting errors (up to 17% annually) lead industrial and energy companies to significant financial losses due to deviation penalties, while also reducing the efficiency of operational planning for generation regimes and regional power grids [2, 3].

&emsp;&emsp;Under modern conditions of dynamic demand and external influences, traditional statistical autoregressive models (ARIMA, SARIMAX) often yield to machine learning algorithms (Gradient Boosting) and modern deep learning architectures (Transformers). This study compares four modern machine learning modeling methods: CatBoost, Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT), N-HiTS, and PatchTST, evaluated on pattern recovery and electricity consumption forecasting quality using real data from a Russian power utility company.

&emsp;&emsp;Modeling and forecasting hourly electricity consumption for decision-making in regional power grid management represents a scientific-applied problem at the intersection of systems analysis, machine learning, time-series analysis, and power engineering. The complexity of selecting an optimal mathematical model stems from the non-stationary, multimodal, and stochastic nature of load data, which is influenced by numerous independent variables ($X$): from meteorological conditions and calendar factors to economic activity and electricity consumption behavior patterns. However, we adhere to a scientific philosophy in which the mathematical essence and utility of a model lie in using fewer parameters than observed data points. If a mathematical model with a small number of parameters explains most observations while remaining useful, adequate, and accurate, it represents a major scientific achievement. Researchers must systematically verify on which data a model succeeds or fails, demonstrating that no single universal model exists.

&emsp;&emsp;This is precisely why researchers worldwide continue to investigate, develop, and compare various mathematical models and methods—from statistical and ensemble techniques (ARIMA, Gradient Boosting) to modern deep learning architectures (LSTM, Transformers, hybrid models)—in search of the most accurate and robust solutions for specific datasets. Even a slight error reduction by fractions of a percent (e.g., reducing MAPE from 2.5% to 2.0%) yields a massive economic impact, especially for major mining, metallurgical enterprises, and data centers. For large industrial enterprises, this translates into optimized electricity procurement costs and reduced non-compliance penalties; for grid and generation companies—enhanced reliability, efficient dispatch planning, and lower reserve capacity costs; for data centers (DCs)—direct reductions in operational expenses and improved energy efficiency [4].

&emsp;&emsp;For large industrial enterprises operating on the WECM (such as mining and metallurgical complexes, chemical and automotive plants producing energy-intensive goods, as well as agricultural holdings), electricity consumption is one of the largest cost items in product prime cost. An accurate hourly forecast for the next day enables such companies to participate effectively in the WECM, entering into optimal contracts and minimizing costs associated with procuring necessary energy. Forecasting errors result either in buying deficit power at inflated spot prices on the balancing market (BM) or selling excess energy at unfavorable rates. To encourage accurate forecasting, the System Operator of the Unified Power System of Russia (SO UPS) imposes financial penalties for deviations of forecasted values from actual consumption [5].

&emsp;&emsp;For energy utilities, precise hourly forecasting forms the foundation of safe and cost-effective power system operations across regions. Energy retail companies use forecasts to construct volume requirements for regional power procurement. Grid companies rely on forecasts to plan maintenance, detect power losses (technical and commercial), and manage grid loads to prevent failures (predictive analytics). For the System Operator of the Russian UPS, aggregate electricity demand forecasting is fundamental for formulating daily power plant dispatch schedules, ensuring frequency stability, and calculating required spinning reserves. Underestimating demand can trigger power shortages and consumer load shedding, whereas overestimating leads to unnecessarily expensive commitment of excess generation capacity (e.g., gas turbines), incurring national-level financial losses worth billions of rubles [6, 7].

&emsp;&emsp;With the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence technologies, data centers (DCs) have become some of the largest consumers of electricity, with power supply costs reaching 40–50% of total operational expenses. Accurate hourly load forecasting is crucial for data centers in both short-term operational and long-term strategic management. It allows optimizing cooling system performance (the most energy-intensive component after IT equipment) and effectively mobilizing backup power sources. In the future, driven by stringent green energy standards, data centers will aim for maximum integration with local renewable energy sources (solar panels, wind turbines), where self-consumption forecasting will become an indispensable requirement for microgrid balancing and carbon footprint reduction [8–10].

&emsp;&emsp;The novelty of the problem statement in this work lies in a comprehensive, comparative, and systems analysis of modern heterogeneous mathematical models for electricity consumption forecasting. These models are developed using diverse machine learning and deep learning methodologies applied to real-world operational data from an energy company in the Central Region of Russia.

<h2 align="center" style="border-bottom: none; border: none;">MACHINE AND DEEP LEARNING METHODS USED TO DEVELOP CANDIDATE MATHEMATICAL MODELS FOR HOURLY LOAD FORECASTING FOR THE NEXT DAY</h2>

### Justification of Model Selection
&emsp;&emsp;To solve the hourly Short-Term Load Forecasting (STLF) problem, we selected state-of-the-art methods spanning distinct machine learning and deep learning paradigms:
- **CatBoost** – selected for its high efficiency in handling categorical and temporal features [15];
- **TFT (Temporal Fusion Transformer)** – selected for its capability to interpret temporal dynamics and capture complex interactions between static and dynamic variables [16];
- **N-HiTS (Neural Hierarchical Interpolation for Time Series)** – selected as a modern architecture that reduces computational complexity via hierarchical interpolation, making it suitable for multi-step forecasting [17];
- **PatchTST** – selected due to its patch-based segmentation of time series, enabling Transformers to better extract local semantic dependencies [18].

&emsp;&emsp;This selection is grounded in the necessity to:
- Account for non-linear relationships between load, temperature, and calendar variables;
- Capture multi-period temporal dependencies in data;
- Ensure forecast interpretability and reliability.

### Decision Tree Gradient Boosting (CatBoost)
&emsp;&emsp;CatBoost [19] represents the gradient boosted decision trees (GBDT) family, widely applied in systems analysis and tabular data processing. For load forecasting, its primary advantages include:
- Automatic handling of categorical features—efficient processing of critical variables such as hour of day, day of week, day type (weekday/weekend/holiday), and month, capturing daily and weekly seasonal patterns;
- Robustness against overfitting—achieved via ordered boosting and built-in regularization;
- Interpretability—feature importance evaluation (Feature Importance) allows validating physical and economic model soundness (e.g., verifying the impact of temperature and historical peak loads).

&emsp;&emsp;Let us review the model update and gradient evaluation scheme in CatBoost [19].

&emsp;&emsp;Given a sample dataset $\{(X_k, Y_k)\}_{k=1}^n$ ordered according to a random permutation $\sigma$, and total trees $I$. Let $M_i(X)$ denote the model after the $i$-th training step for instance $X$. Initialize $M_i(X) = 0$ for all $i = 1, \dots, n$. At each iteration $\text{iter} = 1, \dots, I$, loss function gradients $L(y, a)$ with respect to previous model predictions are computed for each object $i = 1, \dots, n$. For all $j = 1, \dots, i - 1$, the gradient is given by Formula 1:

$$
g_j = \left. \frac{\partial L(y_j, a)}{\partial a} \right|_{a = M_i(X_j)}
$$

&emsp;&emsp;Next, a decision tree $M$ is constructed on the set of pairs $\{(X_j, g_j)\}_{j=1}^{i-1}$ using the gradient boosting tree building algorithm. Subsequently, the model for object $i$ is updated according to:

$$
M_i \leftarrow M_i + M
$$

&emsp;&emsp;Upon completion of all iterations, the algorithm outputs models $M_1, \dots, M_n$ and predictions $M_1(X_1), M_2(X_2), \dots, M_n(X_n)$ [19].

### Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT)
&emsp;&emsp;The TFT model [20] is a state-of-the-art deep learning neural architecture designed specifically for interpretable multi-horizon time series forecasting. TFT explicitly categorizes and processes known future exogenous variables $X$ (such as calendar features) and observed historical factors (lagged load values, ambient temperature). This structure naturally fits scenarios where future ambient temperatures for the forecast horizon are obtainable from meteorological forecasts. Its attention mechanism enables direct pairwise temporal dependency modeling across arbitrary historical time steps, outperforming recurrent networks on long sequences. TFT delivers feature-level and time-step interpretability, revealing which historical windows and features contributed most to a specific forecast [20].

### PatchTST (GluonTS)
&emsp;&emsp;PatchTST represents a breakthrough approach adapting Transformer architectures to time series via patching. Unlike TFT, PatchTST is optimized for univariate time series, achieving high accuracy through two core techniques: segmenting time series into sub-series patches and employing channel independence (forecasting each channel independently). This enables deep extraction of intrinsic temporal dynamics in load series. The approach demonstrates superior performance on large target variable datasets, frequently outperforming classical deep learning methods [21].

### N-HiTS
&emsp;&emsp;N-HiTS is a novel hierarchical architecture based on fully connected networks (FCNs) designed specifically for long-horizon time series forecasting. The model utilizes multiple stacks, each learning to forecast trends at different sampling rates (multi-rate resolution) and interpolation layers. This enables highly efficient modeling of multi-period seasonality (daily, weekly, annual) within data matrices. N-HiTS achieves high forecast accuracy at significantly reduced computational cost and parameter count compared to Transformer architectures, which is essential for operational STLF [17].

&emsp;&emsp;The statistical analysis of real-world data confirms the complex, cyclic, and exogenous-dependent nature of electricity consumption series. The selected ensemble of CatBoost, TFT, PatchTST, and N-HiTS provides a scientifically sound and comprehensive foundation that:
- Leverages distinct architectural strengths (gradient boosting, self-attention Transformers, hierarchical FCNs);
- Enables rigorous comparative evaluation to identify the optimal model for a specific power delivery group;
- Ensures forecast system robustness through model selection and ensembling;
- Provides both point predictions and factor interpretability.

<h2 align="center" style="border-bottom: none; border: none;">DATASET SPLITTING AND REGRESSION MODEL TRAINING</h2>

&emsp;&emsp;The dataset was split chronologically to prevent look-ahead bias (data leakage):
- **Training set (train)** – initial ~90% of observations;
- **Validation set (validation)** – subsequent 9% for hyperparameter tuning and model validation;
- **Test set (test)** – final 168 hours (24h, 72h, 168h windows) for out-of-sample performance evaluation and forecast accuracy testing.

### Hyperparameter Tuning
&emsp;&emsp;A forecast horizon of 168 hours was selected experimentally across all models. Training epochs ranged from 20 to 60. Accuracy evaluation used RMSE as the loss function. Table 1 lists optimal model hyperparameters and descriptions.

&emsp;&emsp;**CatBoost (Gradient Boosting).** Used as the primary baseline solution. Tree depth ($\text{Depth} = 6$) was selected to balance bias and variance; deeper trees ($\text{Depth} = 8\text{--}10$) exhibited rapid overfitting. Iterations (1,000) provided loss convergence at a learning rate ($\text{Learning Rate}$) of 0.03.

&emsp;&emsp;**N-HiTS (Neural Hierarchical Interpolation).** Relies on hierarchical forecasting via multi-rate stacks. Pooling sizes ([8, 4, 1]) form the critical hyperparameter. The first block ($\text{kernel size} = 8$) extracts overall trend; the second (4) captures mid-term variations; the third (1) processes high-frequency details. MLP width (512) enables approximating complex non-linear functions.

| Model | Hyperparameter | Value | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **CatBoost** | Iterations | 1000 | Number of ensemble trees |

&emsp;&emsp;**TFT (Temporal Fusion Transformer).** Hybrid architecture combining self-attention and LSTMs. Hidden Size (64) was intentionally bounded to 64: scaling to 128 or 256 increased training time exponentially without accuracy gains. LSTM Layers (2) extract local temporal dynamics prior to self-attention blocks.

<h2 align="center" style="border-bottom: none; border: none;">QUALITY EVALUATION CRITERIA AND MODEL SELECTION</h2>

<h2 align="center" style="border-bottom: none; border: none;">STATISTICAL SYSTEM ANALYSIS OF RESIDUALS AND DIAGNOSTICS</h2>

&emsp;&emsp;CatBoost residual analysis was performed using Formula (2):

$$
\text{Residuals} = Y_{\text{forecast}} - Y_{\text{actual}}
$$

&emsp;&emsp;Statistical diagnostic properties over the 168-hour test week yielded:
1. **Systematic Bias** – Mean residual equals 8.14 MWh. Relative to mean consumption (>4,600 MWh), forecast bias is under 0.2%, indicating no systematic over- or under-estimation.
2. **Error Variance** – Residual standard deviation ($\sigma$) equals 50.16 MWh, defining confidence interval bounds.
3. **Distribution Shape:**
   - *Skewness* – -0.0283 (near-zero, proving symmetric error distribution).
   - *Kurtosis* – 0.0751 (near-zero, confirming Gaussian tail behavior without extreme error spikes).

&emsp;&emsp;The residual histogram (Fig. 10) visualizes a Gaussian bell curve centered near zero.

  <img src="Photo/plots_diagnostics/9_residuals_hist.png" alt="Fig. 10. Residual histogram">

</div>

&emsp;&emsp;Statistical test suites were performed to verify model statistical validity.

&emsp;&emsp;**Normality Test.** The Shapiro-Wilk test yielded $W = 0.9871$, $p\text{-value} = 0.1249$. Since $p > 0.05$, the null hypothesis of residual normality cannot be rejected. The Q-Q Plot (Fig. 11) shows residual points closely adhering to the theoretical line, confirming Gaussian distribution assumptions.

However, given low overall error ($\text{MAPE } 0.68\%$) and confirmed residual normality, residual autocorrelation does not impede real-world operational deployment.

<h2 align="center" style="border-bottom: none; border: none;">CONCLUSION</h2>

&emsp;&emsp;This paper performed a systematic statistical analysis of modern machine learning and AI methods for developing and tuning hourly load forecasting models. Hyperparameter setups were empirically established for real utility data. Rigorous mathematical evidence justifies CatBoost selection among candidate models.

&emsp;&emsp;The solution addresses essential market bidding requirements for industrial energy consumers on wholesale markets (WECM) and regional grid dispatching, delivering substantial cost savings by reducing forecast errors to under 1%.

&emsp;&emsp;CatBoost proves to be a highly effective AI tool for hourly load forecasting applications.

&emsp;&emsp;Multi-criteria evaluation demonstrates that complex forecasting tasks can be streamlined to CatBoost utilization. All theoretical conclusions are validated empirically.

&emsp;&emsp;Future work will expand model candidate pools and refine data preprocessing pipelines for dynamic adaptation.
